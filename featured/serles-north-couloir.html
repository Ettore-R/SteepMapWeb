Serles North Couloir

The Serles North Couloir is one of those tours that has a bit of everything: good skiing, a proper mountaineering feel and, if you want it, a summit on top. The north-facing couloir generally holds good snow, and the line itself is around 45°, with some sections getting a little steeper depending on the conditions.

The usual approach takes you up to the Serlesjoch and then onwards to the summit via the normal route. The summit is worth doing if you want to make a full day of it, but it can also be skipped. Especially on busy days, to avoid the potential bottleneck of the rappels.

The excitement starts traversing on steep terrain below the summit to enter the couloir. There is one mandatory rappel, with a second rappel further down depending on the amount of snow in the couloir. The first anchor is easy to spot, located on a boulder at the top of the couloir and equipped with slings. Around 50–100 m lower, on the left side, there is a second anchor which has recently been bolted.

In a good snow year, with plenty of filling in the couloir, the second rappel can be avoided by traversing out to the right and rejoining the couloir lower down. With less snow, or if the traverse doesn't go, expect to use both rappel stations. A 60 m rope is the standard reference for the individual rappels; but it is possible also with a shouter one.

Once you're back on the skis, the fun really starts. The couloir is steep but generally wide enough to make proper turns, and the north aspect can offer excellent snow. Below the main couloir, the terrain gradually opens up and the descent continues for a long way, making this much more than just a quick steep-skiing lap!
